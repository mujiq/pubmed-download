Appendix C — Clinical Screeners & PROs

The validated, standardized patient-reported-outcome (PRO) instruments and clinical screeners HikmaEngine administers — full item text, scoring and band cut-offs. These are the literature-validated instruments (PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT-C, ISI…), distinct from the broad lifestyle/condition Question bank (E) and the Lifestyles (F) model — the bank's validated_by links escalate a crude item to the matching instrument here. They are the LIFE stream (Doc 07 §1) and the MCS/behavioural markers of Doc 02.

Illustrative
All bands, weights and cut-points on this page are literature/guideline-anchored examples and must be re-verified and cohort-adjusted before any production use (Conventions §5.6). This is design documentation, not a validated medical device.

26 instruments across mental-health, sleep, respiratory/atopy, metabolic/activity, substance, frailty, cognition and sex-specific domains.

PHQ-9 — Patient Health Questionnaire (depression)

MCScadence: quarterly / on-triggersource: Kroenke 2001 (public domain)

Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by any of the following problems?

Response scale: 0 = not at all · 1 = several days · 2 = more than half the days · 3 = nearly every day

  1. Little interest or pleasure in doing things
  2. Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless
  3. Trouble falling or staying asleep, or sleeping too much
  4. Feeling tired or having little energy
  5. Poor appetite or overeating
  6. Feeling bad about yourself — or that you are a failure
  7. Trouble concentrating on things
  8. Moving or speaking slowly, or being fidgety/restless
  9. Thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself
ScoringSum 0–27. 0–4 none/minimal · 5–9 mild · 10–14 moderate · 15–19 mod-severe · 20–27 severe.
Bandsgreen 0–4 · yellow 5–14 · red ≥15
SafetyItem 9 > 0 forces MCS red/critical irrespective of total and fires the crisis pathway (Doc 16). Never averaged away.

PHQ-2 — ultra-brief depression screen

MCScadence: Core (every visit)source: Kroenke 2003

Over the last 2 weeks, how often bothered by…

Response scale: 0 = not at all · 1 = several days · 2 = more than half the days · 3 = nearly every day

  1. Little interest or pleasure in doing things
  2. Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless
ScoringSum 0–6; ≥3 → administer full PHQ-9.
Bands≥3 triggers PHQ-9
SafetyPositive screen escalates to PHQ-9.

GAD-7 — Generalized Anxiety Disorder

MCScadence: quarterly / on-triggersource: Spitzer 2006 (public domain)

Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following problems?

Response scale: 0 = not at all · 1 = several days · 2 = more than half the days · 3 = nearly every day

  1. Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge
  2. Not being able to stop or control worrying
  3. Worrying too much about different things
  4. Trouble relaxing
  5. Being so restless that it is hard to sit still
  6. Becoming easily annoyed or irritable
  7. Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen
ScoringSum 0–21. 0–4 minimal · 5–9 mild · 10–14 moderate · 15–21 severe.
Bandsgreen 0–4 · yellow 5–14 · red ≥15

GAD-2 — ultra-brief anxiety screen

MCScadence: Core (every visit)source: Kroenke 2007

Over the last 2 weeks, how often bothered by…

Response scale: 0 = not at all · 1 = several days · 2 = more than half the days · 3 = nearly every day

  1. Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge
  2. Not being able to stop or control worrying
ScoringSum 0–6; ≥3 → administer full GAD-7.
Bands≥3 triggers GAD-7

AUDIT-C — alcohol use

HEP / MET / MCScadence: Coresource: Bush 1998 (WHO)

Alcohol consumption screen (WHO).

Response scale: item-specific 0–4

  1. How often do you have a drink containing alcohol? (0 never … 4 ≥4×/week)
  2. How many standard drinks on a typical drinking day? (0:1–2 … 4:≥10)
  3. How often do you have ≥6 drinks on one occasion? (0 never … 4 daily/almost)
ScoringSum 0–12.
Bandsat-risk ≥4 M / ≥3 F
SafetyHigh scores route to brief intervention + LFTs.

PSS-4 — Perceived Stress Scale (short)

MCS / ENDOcadence: quarterlysource: Cohen 1983

In the last month, how often have you felt…

Response scale: 0 never … 4 very often

  1. …unable to control the important things in your life?
  2. …confident about your ability to handle personal problems? (reverse)
  3. …that things were going your way? (reverse)
  4. …difficulties piling up so high you could not overcome them?
ScoringReverse items 2–3, sum 0–16; higher = more stress.
Bandsgreen low · yellow moderate · red high

UCLA-3 — loneliness / social connection

MCScadence: quarterlysource: Hughes 2004

How often do you feel…

Response scale: 1 hardly ever · 2 some of the time · 3 often

  1. …that you lack companionship?
  2. …left out?
  3. …isolated from others?
ScoringSum 3–9; ≥6 ≈ lonely.
Bandsgreen ≤4 · yellow 5 · red ≥6

WHO-5 — Well-Being Index

MCScadence: quarterlysource: WHO 1998

Over the last 2 weeks…

Response scale: 0 at no time … 5 all of the time

  1. I have felt cheerful and in good spirits
  2. I have felt calm and relaxed
  3. I have felt active and vigorous
  4. I woke up feeling fresh and rested
  5. My daily life has been filled with things that interest me
ScoringSum ×4 → 0–100; <50 low well-being, <28 screen for depression.
Bandsgreen ≥50 · yellow 28–49 · red <28
Safety<28 → PHQ-9.

ISI — Insomnia Severity Index

SLPcadence: on-triggersource: Morin 2011

Rate the severity of your insomnia problems in the last 2 weeks.

Response scale: 0–4 per item

  1. Difficulty falling asleep
  2. Difficulty staying asleep
  3. Problem waking too early
  4. Satisfaction with current sleep pattern
  5. Interference with daily functioning
  6. Noticeability to others of impairment
  7. Worry/distress about sleep
ScoringSum 0–28.
Bands0–7 none · 8–14 sub-threshold · 15–21 moderate · 22–28 severe
SafetyPairs with overnight SpO₂/ODI → OSA referral.

IPAQ-SF — activity (self-report, cross-checks wearable)

FITcadence: Coresource: IPAQ 2002

In the last 7 days, time spent in vigorous, moderate, and walking activity (+ sitting).

Response scale: days/week × min/day

  1. Vigorous-intensity days & minutes
  2. Moderate-intensity days & minutes
  3. Walking days & minutes
  4. Sitting time on a weekday
ScoringConvert to MET-min/week; map to MVPA band.
Bands≥150 min MVPA green
SafetySelf-report cross-checked vs wearable steps/MVPA for plausibility (Doc 06).

Diet-quality screener (Mediterranean / HEI-style)

NUT / METcadence: Coresource: PREDIMED-style

Usual weekly intake across food groups.

Response scale: frequency per item

  1. Vegetables & fruit servings/day
  2. Whole grains vs refined
  3. Legumes/nuts per week
  4. Fish per week
  5. Olive oil as main fat
  6. Red/processed meat per week
  7. Sugary drinks/day
  8. Ultra-processed food frequency
ScoringComposite 0–14 (Mediterranean) → diet-quality band.
Bandshigh / moderate / poor

Smoking / nicotine / shisha (cross-cutting modifier)

CV · HEM · INFcadence: Coresource: Doc 02

Tobacco / nicotine / waterpipe use.

Response scale: status + pack-years

  1. Current status (never / former / current)
  2. Cigarettes/day & pack-years
  3. Waterpipe (shisha) sessions/week
  4. Vaping/nicotine pouches
  5. Cotinine (if available, objective)
ScoringRisk multiplier on CV, HEM/respiratory, INF (Doc 02 modifiers).
Bandsnever / former / current
SafetyCurrent use raises CV/respiratory weighting and screening.

TNSS — Total Nasal Symptom Score (allergic rhinitis)

INFcadence: on-triggersource: Downie 2004 (illustrative)

Rate each nasal symptom over the past 24 h / typical day.

Response scale: 0 none … 3 severe per symptom

  1. Nasal congestion
  2. Rhinorrhoea (runny nose)
  3. Sneezing
  4. Nasal itching
ScoringSum 0–12.
Bands0–3 mild · 4–7 moderate · 8–12 severe

ACQ — Asthma Control Questionnaire

INFcadence: on-triggersource: Juniper 1999

Over the past week…

Response scale: 0 (none) … 6 (severe) per item; reliever item banded

  1. Night waking from asthma
  2. Symptoms on waking
  3. Activity limitation
  4. Shortness of breath
  5. Wheeze
  6. Reliever puffs/day
ScoringMean of items 0–6.
Bands<0.75 well-controlled · 0.75–1.5 grey · ≥1.5 uncontrolled
SafetyUncontrolled → clinician review; pairs with peak-flow.

POEM — Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure

INFcadence: on-triggersource: Charman 2004

Over the last week, how many days did your skin…

Response scale: 0 no days … 4 every day per item

  1. Itch
  2. Disturb sleep
  3. Bleed
  4. Weep/ooze
  5. Crack
  6. Flake
  7. Feel dry/rough
ScoringSum 0–28.
Bands0–2 clear · 3–7 mild · 8–16 moderate · 17–24 severe · 25–28 very severe

MRC Dyspnoea Scale (breathlessness)

CV · HEMcadence: on-triggersource: Fletcher 1959 (mMRC)

Which best describes your breathlessness?

Response scale: grade 1–5

  1. 1 — only on strenuous exertion
  2. 2 — hurrying / slight hill
  3. 3 — walks slower / stops on the level
  4. 4 — stops after ~100 m
  5. 5 — too breathless to leave the house / on dressing
ScoringSingle grade 1–5.
Bands3–5 significant functional limitation
SafetyGrade ≥4 with desaturation → expedited review (pairs with SpO₂).

EPDS — Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale

MCScadence: pregnancy / postpartumsource: Cox 1987

In the past 7 days (perinatal mood)…

Response scale: 0–3 per item (10 items)

  1. Able to laugh / see the funny side
  2. Looked forward with enjoyment
  3. Blamed myself unnecessarily
  4. Anxious or worried
  5. Scared / panicky
  6. Things getting on top of me
  7. Unhappy → difficulty sleeping
  8. Sad or miserable
  9. So unhappy I have been crying
  10. Thoughts of harming myself
ScoringSum 0–30.
Bandsgreen <10 · yellow 10–12 · red ≥13 (likely depression)
SafetyItem 10 > 0 (self-harm) forces MCS red/critical and fires the crisis pathway (Doc 16), like PHQ-9 item 9.

Mini-Cog / cognitive screen (age-adjusted)

MCScadence: annual ≥65 / on-triggersource: Borson 2000

Three-word recall + clock-drawing test.

Response scale: recall 0–3 + clock 0 or 2

  1. Register & recall 3 words
  2. Draw a clock to a set time (normal = 2)
  3. Total = recall + clock
Scoring0–5.
Bands≥3 likely normal · <3 screen-positive → MoCA/MMSE + clinician
SafetyScreen-positive routes to formal cognitive assessment, never an autonomous diagnosis.

PHQ-15 — somatic symptom burden

MCScadence: quarterly / on-triggersource: Kroenke 2002

Over the last 4 weeks, bothered by… (15 somatic symptoms)

Response scale: 0 not bothered … 2 bothered a lot

  1. Stomach pain
  2. Back pain
  3. Pain in arms/legs/joints
  4. Headaches
  5. Chest pain
  6. Dizziness
  7. Palpitations
  8. Shortness of breath
  9. Bowel symptoms
  10. Fatigue / low energy
  11. Trouble sleeping
ScoringSum 0–30.
Bands0–4 minimal · 5–9 low · 10–14 medium · 15–30 high
SafetyHigh somatic burden cross-checks MCS and the relevant organ pillars.

DAST-10 — Drug Abuse Screening Test

MCScadence: on-triggersource: Skinner 1982

In the past 12 months, regarding non-medical drug use…

Response scale: yes / no (10 items)

  1. Used more than intended
  2. Unable to stop
  3. Neglected obligations
  4. Guilt about use
  5. Withdrawal symptoms
  6. Medical / social / legal problems from use
ScoringSum 0–10.
Bands0 none · 1–2 low · 3–5 moderate · 6–8 substantial · 9–10 severe
SafetyModerate+ routes to brief intervention / referral.

AUDIT — Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test (full)

HEP · MCScadence: on-trigger (AUDIT-C positive)source: Saunders 1993 (WHO)

Full alcohol screen when AUDIT-C is positive.

Response scale: 0–4 per item (10 items)

  1. AUDIT-C consumption items 1–3
  2. Impaired control over drinking
  3. Failed normal expectations
  4. Morning drinking
  5. Guilt after drinking
  6. Blackouts
  7. Injury from drinking
  8. Others concerned
ScoringSum 0–40.
Bands0–7 low · 8–15 hazardous · 16–19 harmful · ≥20 likely dependence
Safety≥20 → assess dependence/withdrawal; LFTs; brief intervention.

SARC-F — sarcopenia / frailty screen

BCMcadence: annual (≥65)source: Malmstrom 2013

Difficulty with strength, ambulation, rising, stairs, falls.

Response scale: 0–2 per item

  1. Lifting / carrying ~4.5 kg
  2. Walking across a room
  3. Rising from a chair / bed
  4. Climbing 10 stairs
  5. Falls in the past year
ScoringSum 0–10.
Bands≥4 suggests sarcopenia → grip / gait + ALMI (Doc 02 BCM)
SafetyPositive routes to functional assessment + resistance-training plan.

PSQI — Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index

SLPcadence: on-triggersource: Buysse 1989

Sleep quality over the last month (7 components).

Response scale: 7 components, 0–3 each

  1. Subjective sleep quality
  2. Sleep latency
  3. Sleep duration
  4. Habitual efficiency
  5. Disturbances
  6. Sleep-medication use
  7. Daytime dysfunction
ScoringGlobal 0–21.
Bands>5 = poor sleep quality
SafetyPairs with ISI + wearable sleep; high score + snoring → STOP-BANG / OSA.

STOP-BANG — OSA risk screen

SLPcadence: on-triggersource: Chung 2008

Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnoea, Pressure (BP), BMI, Age, Neck, Gender.

Response scale: yes / no (8 items)

  1. Loud Snoring
  2. Daytime Tiredness
  3. Observed apnoea
  4. high blood Pressure
  5. BMI > 35
  6. Age > 50
  7. Neck > 40 cm
  8. male sex
ScoringSum 0–8.
Bands0–2 low · 3–4 intermediate · 5–8 high OSA risk
SafetyHigh risk → overnight SpO₂/ODI or HSAT (Doc 02 SLP); cross-checks ISI.

IIEF-5 — erectile function (male)

ENDOcadence: on-triggersource: Rosen 1999 (male)

Over the past 6 months (male sexual function)…

Response scale: 1–5 per item (5 items)

  1. Confidence in getting an erection
  2. Erections firm enough for penetration
  3. Maintaining after penetration
  4. Maintaining to completion
  5. Satisfaction with intercourse
ScoringSum 5–25.
Bands22–25 none · 17–21 mild · 12–16 mild-mod · 8–11 moderate · 5–7 severe ED
SafetyED can be an early CV/endothelial and low-testosterone marker — cross-check CV/ENDO.

MENQOL — Menopause-specific Quality of Life (female)

ENDOcadence: on-triggersource: Hilditch 1996 (female)

Bother from menopausal symptoms across four domains.

Response scale: Likert; higher = more bothersome

  1. Vasomotor (hot flushes, sweats)
  2. Psychosocial
  3. Physical
  4. Sexual
ScoringDomain mean scores (higher = worse QoL).
Bandsdescriptor by domain
SafetySevere vasomotor/sleep impact → ENDO/SLP attention; HRT decision is clinician-led.
